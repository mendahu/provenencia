# Deployment plan — Spike 16

Spike 16 is an odds-and-ends spike ([odd and even spikes](../README.md#odd-and-even-spikes)): small fixes, UX refinements and follow-ups, in batches, each a slice with its own Check. Batch 1 takes the high and medium findings of the [Person page audit](person-page-audit.md) (2026-11-08), an area audit of Spike 13's Person page with all five [`review-pr`](../../../.agents/skills/review-pr/SKILL.md) lenses. Findings that edit the same code or the same concern share a PR (user, 2026-11-08), unless the map review found a reason to keep them apart ([review](deployment-plan.review.md)); the rest are parked in idea files ([Where the notes went](#where-the-notes-went)). Batch 2 (2026-11-08) reworks the catalog's caches so the conclusion layer's code gets simpler: entities and indexes held in Go memory and updated exactly on commit, answers composed from them, and Swift following what each value was built from ([design](cache-architecture.md)). A last pass the same day combined PRs with heavy domain or file overlap to cut review cycles, from 33 to 25 ([review](deployment-plan.review.md#consolidation-2026-11-08)). Batch 3 (2026-11-10) quiets the Swift test runs: no window on screen and no project opened by the test host. Batch 5 (2026-11-16) adds an event's **As of** date: a day a lasting event was known to be under way, kept beside its start and end and read as a sample, never as when it happened ([design](event-as-of.md)). Batch 6 (2026-11-17) takes parked ideas one PR at a time; the first is the graph delete confirm naming the Artifacts it leaves in no Evidence graph ([design](graph-delete-leaves-no-graph.md)). Batch 7 (2026-11-18) lands ahead of Batch 6: the app opens one workspace window, and what was built for a second window goes ([rules](#batch-7--one-window); the long-term idea is parked in [`ideas/multiple-windows-and-sync.md`](../../ideas/multiple-windows-and-sync.md)).

**A brief covers one view.** A PR that adds, changes or removes chrome waits on a brief for each view it touches. Briefs and PR plans are written just before the PR ([`add-design-brief`](../../../.agents/skills/add-design-brief/SKILL.md), [`plan-pr`](../../../.agents/skills/plan-pr/SKILL.md)). A bugfix or a behavior change with no new chrome is not gated.

**Batch 1 — Person page audit fixes** (user, 2026-11-08; [audit](person-page-audit.md)):

1. **A burial-only life reads in order.** A person known only from a parish burial reads Birth unknown, the undated events, Death unknown, then the burial, in the list and on the lifeline. A person with a dated baptism and burial and no death has Death unknown just before the burial, on the list and on the lifeline, so the life between them is drawn before the death.
2. **A crafted project cannot stall the Person page.** A project holding an event with 10,000 places, a person with 20,000 children, a person with 50,000 parents, and 20,000 of one person's records in one Evidence graph opens, and each Person section draws in under a second.
3. **A long list unfolds a page at a time.** A person with 2,000 events, one of them with 500 participants, opens on Timeline without a stall; each Show more, in any section, reveals at most a page of 50 and moves focus to the first item it reveals; every event still has its point on the lifeline. Hovering a graph on the record links diagram whose record line is folded lights up that Source's Show more, and editing one of the person's event dates, then pressing Back to the person, rescales the lifeline to the new range (reworded for one window, Batch 7, 2026-11-18).
4. **Provenance shows today's words.** Rename a Source, or fix a Citation's label or page: the Vitals provenance sheet, the Gallery captions and the likeness viewer's record row show the new words, as the Sources section does.
5. **A failed reload says so.** When a section's reload fails, its rows stay and a notice with Try again sits above them; a first load that fails offers Try again too; VoiceOver hears both.
6. **The diagram says when it is partial.** A person whose records were too many to compare, or too many to draw, shows a note in the diagram column instead of drawing every link thin or dropping graphs silently.
7. **Keyboard and VoiceOver keep their place.** ⌘1–⌘5 announces the section and leaves focus on the selected segment; removing a record that keeps the person is announced; Show more in Family, Timeline and Sources moves focus to the first item it reveals (bar 3).
8. **One word for a person's records.** The Person page's header counts "7 records" (the Event and Place headers too), the Remove confirm ends "…, and the person stays", and a person whose last record is unpromoted from an Evidence graph leaves, when Back returns to their page, with "This person no longer exists" (reworded for one window, Batch 7, 2026-11-18).

**Batch 2 — Cache architecture** (user, 2026-11-08; [design](cache-architecture.md)):

9. **No cache empties itself on any write.** After an Observation of a filed person is edited, the next Promote proposal and the Family section reuse the kinship pivots and the match index instead of rebuilding them; `rg DropOnHandleCommit core` finds nothing, and Verify (`PROVENENCIA_GRAPH_VERIFY=1`) checks every cache.
10. **Promote's cost follows the Source, not the catalog.** On the large fixture at 16,000 persons a warm proposal is at least three times faster than today's on the same machine, the first proposal after a handle commit is no slower than 1.75 times a warm one (1.5 until 2026-11-20, raised for the Blacksmith CI runners, user), and neither more than doubles from 4,000 to 16,000 persons. Its suggestions are the same as today's.
11. **A Person page opens from memory.** With the catalog warm, opening a Person on Vitals, its provenance sheet, its Gallery and its Sources section issues no SQL but the one read of its pictures' file paths (user, 2026-11-13: one query, rule "No Go cache for a view that is one query"); a Source rename shows on every page without reloading any Evidence graph.
12. **Lists and progress never rescan the catalog.** The Sources list's graph progress needs no full-table count; the Sources section of a person in 80 Evidence graphs loads no whole graph; a write updates only the rows it touched in the Persons, Events and Places lists.
13. **Walks are lookups.** A city's place chain reads without loading its 6,000 location links; the Family section reads in one pass; the life rows and the Timeline take their owners and participants from summary rows.
14. **Back is instant.** Returning to a Person, Event or Place page shows its last value at once while it refreshes; a write that touches nothing a cached section was built from leaves it alone.
15. **One table names every cache.** `docs/catalog-caches.md` (new in S16-10) lists every Go and Swift cache with what builds it and the signal that updates it; at 16,000 persons the Go caches together stay under 250 MB of live data, and no cache grows faster than the catalog; every value the app derives from cached data is built once per change of its inputs.

**Batch 5 — Event as-of dates** (user, 2026-11-16; [design](event-as-of.md)):

16. **An attestation is recorded as one.** On a new project, an obituary's "survived by X of Town Y" of 10 Jan 1990 is recorded on X's residence as **As of** 10 Jan 1990, with any qualifier the date editor offers; a directory's As of 10 Feb 2015 on the same residence sits beside it, and neither the Evidence graph nor the auto-reconciler reads the two as a conflict.
17. **Promote meets two attestations of one residence.** The 1990 obituary record and the 2015 directory record of one residence are compared with no date conflict; a record whose residence closed in 1985, or began in 1995, conflicts with the 1990 one.
18. **Every date line dates an as-of event.** A residence known only from its two as-ofs is dated from 10 Jan 1990 to 10 Feb 2015, open both sides, in the Events list, on its Event page, on the Timeline and on its Evidence graph card, and sorts at 10 Jan 1990; its Event page's Date row lists both as-ofs with their records.
19. **The Timeline draws it.** On the Person page's Timeline, that residence spans 1990 to 2015 on the lifeline with both ends marked open; a start of 1985 closes its left end. A person's birth is never dated by an as-of.

**Batch 6 — Idea follow-ups** (user, 2026-11-17):

20. **A graph delete warns before it leaves an Artifact in no graph.** On a Source with a Default graph and a Jones graph, where Artifact A is linked only to Jones and Artifact B to both, deleting Jones asks to confirm with a sentence naming A, and not B, as no longer in any evidence graph; after the delete, A's row shows the "Not in an evidence graph" badge.
21. **Back keeps your place in a section.** Open a child's tile in Family, then the child's spouse, then press Back twice: each time the section scrolls to the tile you opened, and it has focus. The same holds for a Timeline row and a Sources row. Folds still open closed (S13-16). (P-19, [audit](person-page-audit.md).)
22. **Every count has a plural.** The provenance sheet's "records from N birth events" has plural forms for the events count as well as the records count, and the strings check refuses a count without one, even inside an entry that has other substitutions. (P-18.)
23. **A Properties search with no match looks like every other empty list.** Searching Properties for "zzz" shows the kit's empty state, as the Sources list does when its filters hide everything, with a Clear search button that empties the field and brings the rows back.
24. **One querier type for catalog reads.** `rg -n "[Qq]uerier interface|q interface \{" core` finds one line, `dbsql`'s `Querier`. A new package that declares its own is refused by `add-catalog-query`. Nothing in the app changes.
25. **Every popup closes the same way.** An action menu, a `PVSelect`, a combo box's list, the omnibar's results and the Back/Forward history menu each close on Escape, on a click outside, and when you switch to another app. None closes on the click that opened it. In a menu with a disabled row (the citation composer's Clear menu with no region), typing its first letter, ↑/↓ and Home/End skip it, and Return never closes the menu without acting.
26. **The omnibar's results work like a combo box's list.** In the results, Home/End and Page Up/Down move the highlight, the card matches the combo box's, and the list stays on screen at a narrow window or near the screen edge.
27. **The history menu behaves like every other menu.** Holding Back shows the same rows as today, which move with ↑/↓ and open with Return. Escape, a click outside and an app switch close it, and switching between Back and Forward still works.
28. **Catalog tests make a test image one way.** `rg -n "png.Encode" core/database --glob '*_test.go'` finds no hit outside the shared test helper.
29. **Section counts read alike.** A Source with 3 notes, 3 Artifacts and 3 Evidence graphs shows "3 notes", "3 artifacts" and "3 evidence graphs" in the three section headers. A Person with 3 likenesses shows "3 likenesses" over the Gallery, an Event or a Place "3 pictures", and the citation composer's Citation column "3 citations". A section with none shows no count.
30. **A failed sidebar recount says so.** When the recount after a write fails (adding a Source with the catalog session refusing), the "Couldn't refresh catalog counts" toast shows, as it does when the first count at launch fails, and the badges keep their last values.
31. **A combo box never submits a pick it no longer shows.** On a Source type page, pick Folio in a suggestion picker, clear the field or type "zzz", and press Return: nothing is added and the field keeps the text. Click away and it shows Folio again. With the field showing Folio, Return adds it, as today. The metadata board's inline Add field row behaves the same.
32. **Toasts stack instead of replacing each other.** Choose two portraits in a row: both toasts show, stacked, newest on top, and each leaves five seconds after it appeared. Dismissing the lower one leaves the upper one's timer untouched. A workspace notice and a page's toast never overlap. The same failure twice shows one card whose timer restarts.
33. **Every seed writes through one transaction.** `rg -n "func (Upsert|EnsureBinding|Install)\(c \*database\.Catalog" core/database/properties core/database/propertyterms core/database/subjecttypes core/database/sourcecredibilitygrades core/database/subjectvocab --glob '!*_test.go' --glob '!*test/**'` finds nothing: the subject vocabulary, Properties, terms and credibility grades install through `writes.Run`, and a failed install leaves none of its rows. Nothing in the app changes.
36. **One change builder and one change-only write call.** `rg -n "return struct\{\}\{\}, " core api` and `rg -n "rowchange\.(FullRow|UpdatedRow|DeletedRow)\b" core api` find nothing, and no catalog package builds its own create, update or delete change around its column list. Nothing in the app changes.
35. **A large delete still answers.** Deleting a bridge file whose Subjects are all claimed by one handle works however many Subjects it holds; the delete's Impact is the same as it would be for a few. Nothing else in the app changes.

## Working rules

These are the calls for this spike. PR plans treat them as binding.

### Batch 1 — Person page audit fixes

- **(Batch 1) Crafted fan-out is capped, not engineered** (user, 2026-11-08). A cost that only a crafted or badly broken project reaches gets a fixed cap checked before the work, not a faster algorithm. Real data never reaches a cap. Past a cap a read keeps the first items in the order it already reads them and drops the rest without saying so; the record links read is the exception (rule "The record links read is bounded and says so"). The caps:
  - an event's places, 20, applied in `eventLocations` (S16-01 plan D-6), one event's capped location links, which `placesOfEvents` (the shared read S14-40 added, which `eventPlaceGraph` uses), `leadLife`, `lifeOwners` and `composeEvents` in `conclusionsummaries/compose.go` read, so no read walks the rest; `eventPlaces` takes its caller's links (S16-01);

- **(Batch 5) Every qualifier, no exception** (user, 2026-11-16). An as-of takes any date the editor offers (exact, About, Before, After, a range); the composer has no As of rule. - **(Batch 5) An as-of x reads as "started by x, still under way at x"** (user, 2026-11-16). For every comparison it is the bound pair start ≤ x and end ≥ x, read with the logic start and end already have; it is only stored apart. An as-of's date covers a window of days (ABT widened as matching widens it, a range its two bounds, BEF and AFT open on one side): the event started by the window's last day and was still under way on its first; an open side bounds nothing (`BEF 1990`: started by 1990; `AFT 1990`: still under way after 1990). Consequences, each binding S16-40:
  - Two as-ofs never conflict: any span covering both satisfies both.
  - An as-of conflicts with a closed start after it or a closed end before it, on either side, each closed side on its own (a half-open period still bounds on its closed side).
  - An as-of agrees only when it falls inside the other side's period closed on both ends, counted as the date comparer counts a date inside a period (`contains`, partial); an as-of alone never agrees with another as-of.
  - An as-of against the other side's occurrence `date` follows today's period-against-date reading: as-ofs alone make a period open both ways, which compares as unknown (neither agrees nor conflicts); with a closed start or end, today's period stand-in is compared.
  - No weight of its own: an as-of counts under the event `date` lead's weights (`subject.Weights.event.date`).
- **(Batch 5) A closed as-of counts toward the person's lifespan; it never dates a birth or a death** (user, 2026-11-16). The person was alive on a day an event they take part in was under way, so `subjectprofile`'s lifespan reads the event's closed as-ofs. Birth and death facts (`lifeFrom`) take their date from `date`, else `start_date`, as today. Binds S16-40 and S16-42.
- **(Batch 5) One dating, in Go, derived on read** ([design](event-as-of.md#the-dating-envelope)). Every reader takes an event's four date keys from `subject.Periods` (the lead plus its `Start`, `End` and `Samples`), never from a hand list. Matching (S16-40) compares an as-of with the other side's period stand-in (`period`) through the date comparer, picking the deciding as-ofs with `valuecompare.SampleExtremes`; display (S16-42) adds one pure helper in `core/valuecompare`, typed on date slices, that returns the occurrence, the bounds and the known span with its open sides, reusing `SampleExtremes`. Nothing writes `start_date` or `end_date` from as-ofs.
- **(Batch 5) The engine sends the dating, not the as-ofs** (map review K-3). An event summary carries which dating applies (occurrence, span, as-of), its low and high DateValues and their open sides: two dates however many as-ofs. Swift formats that and no longer decides which key dates an event (`DateRowDisplay.line(date:start:end:)`, `EventDetailContent.dateRow`, `EvidenceGraphSnapshot.dateLine`). Binds S16-42 and S16-44.
- **(Batch 5) As-ofs alone make a span open on both sides** (user, 2026-11-16). An event with only as-ofs displays and sorts from its earliest as-of, spans to its latest, and is drawn open on both sides; a start or an end closes that side. The line's wording for each case is S16-D4's call. Binds S16-42 and S16-44.
