## Goal (dogfood bar)

**By spike close**, each of these is true on the product owner's own test people:

1. **One page, five sections.** The header (name, born/died line, portrait, gender badge) stays put while the user moves between Vitals, Family, Timeline, Gallery and Sources in one action from anywhere on the page.
2. **Every Vitals value explains itself.** Clicking the provenance control beside the birth date opens a sheet listing every record of that date (from the birth event), the auto-reconciler's reasoning, and links that open the Source, the Citation and the exact Observation. A disagreeing value still shows as mixed in the row.
3. **The family is there.** A person with two marriages shows both families, each spouse with their children; their parents; and their siblings, including siblings known only through a shared parent. Every tile shows a likeness, name, birth and death date and place, in the person's gender/sex color, and opens that relative's page. Nobody unpromoted appears.
4. **A life in order.** The Timeline lists every event the person took part in, earliest first, each with its name, date, place, the person's role, the event's picture (else the Event mark; 2026-11-02), and the other canonical participants with their roles. A line drawn to scale runs from birth to death, each dated event points to its place on it, and the gaps in the record show as empty stretches; a birth or death with no record still has its marker ("Birth unknown"); undated events sit together between birth and death (2026-11-02). Every event opens its page, and every participant opens their Timeline.
5. **Likenesses.** The Gallery shows every likeness; one opens a viewer that steps through them; from the gallery or viewer a likeness can be set as the portrait or opened at its Citation.
6. **Sources.** The Sources section lists every identity claim, grouped under its Source (title and type; the date was descoped 2026-10-31) and ordered by Source title, with each claim's Subject card; a census contributing two claims from two graphs shows one group. Each Source opens its page.
7. **Claims.** Removing a claim un-promotes the Subject; removing the last one warns that the person will be deleted, and proceeding lands on the Persons list. (A claim's review became bar 10 on 2026-10-29.)
8. **Matching is unchanged.** Promote proposals are the same as before the spike: a shared parent is not sibling evidence ([R-18a](person-detail.md#2-family)).
9. **Promote only files.** On a Source of 100 new people, Promote asks for a target per row and nothing else: no evidence sheet, no pins, no confidence or argument, and no strong, medium or weak band (the score still ranks the suggestions). Done files the claims and connections, an existing project opens with no `identity_claim_evidence` table, and Identity Claims carry no confidence grade or argument. Every filed row is Accepted. (Added 2026-10-29 for slice 0; the claim fields 2026-10-29; the per-row status descoped 2026-10-30 with S13-30.)
10. **How the records connect.** The Person page shows the person's Evidence graphs around a circle, each labelled with the graph ref the Sources list shows, and an arc between two graphs for each other person both records name: thin when the two records share one fact about that person, thick when they share two or more. A record that shares no one with another stands apart. Hovering a graph on the circle highlights its line in the Sources list, and hovering that line highlights the graph on the circle; nothing else about a line is shown. (Added 2026-10-29 for slice 7; replaces the claim review. The form and the count decided 2026-11-07.)

**Scale** (slice 0, 2026-10-29): Spike 12's — a Source of 100 new people on one Promote page, the 41-Subject obituary, a project whose every accepted claim has pins. Removal only takes work away: Done writes one claim per filed row and its bridges, and no evidence read runs before it. **Slice 7:** a person with 5 – 20 Evidence graphs is typical and 50 is the budget (1,225 pair comparisons per read).

### Slice 7 — How a person's records connect

- **Links between Evidence graphs are computed, never stored** (user, 2026-10-29). A node is an Evidence graph holding an accepted member of the person; a link compares the person's two members in two graphs. Nothing is added to the catalog: no researcher-chosen links and no research log. Each Identity Claim keeps its status (it carries no argument, working rule "Identity Claims carry no confidence and no argument"). Binds S13-26 – S13-28.
- **Scoring and the view are two concerns** (user, 2026-10-29). Scoring the connection between two Evidence graphs and visualizing the connections are specified in [`evidence-graph-comparison.md`](evidence-graph-comparison.md) Requirements 1 and 2 and built separately; the view reads only the scoring's output. The scoring is pure and driven by the match registry like matching, and reads none of graph alignment's weights, band or accept bar. The slice runs last, after the Promote cleanup, the pin removal and the Person page. Binds S13-26 – S13-29.
- **Slice 7 starts with a simple count** (user, 2026-11-07; answers open question 11). The first version is built to be dogfooded on real people before anything richer is designed. It judges two members the researcher has already joined to one entity, which is a different job from Promote's suggestions, so it counts shared facts rather than scoring likeness. Binds S13-26 – S13-28.
  - **A line per shared person.** Two Evidence graphs get one line for each person entity, other than the page's own, with an accepted member in both. Persons only: a shared event or place is not a line, and the page's own person is not one either, since every graph shares it.
  - **A matching Property counts.** Every product Property both members record counts once when `propertycompare.Compare` says agree or partial, not only the Properties Subject compare weighs: a religion or an occupation is evidence on the Subject. Values compare under `subjectcompare.Rules.Meta`, and a pooled Property counts once with its lead (gender with sex at birth). Partial counts, and is read liberally, because the researcher has already joined the two: a name matches when the name compare says agree or partial, so words line up (aliases and fuzzy spellings included) and what is left over is not a second first name or family name ("Amy" / "Amy Lee Anne Long" matches; "John Smith" / "Mary Smith" does not).
  - **A matching link counts.** A link both members record counts once when it has the same `BridgeKey` and its far ends are filed on the same canonical entity: a relationship (to anyone, the page's own person included) or a participation in the same canonical event in the same role (a shared birth, death or marriage). Links come from the shared loader (S13-29) with the implied ones, so a spouse relationship in one record matches a shared marriage event in the other; `kinship.SameShape` keeps a marriage and the spouse link it implies one fact, also when both records write the spouse link down: a pair of links whose shapes pass through pivots filed on one entity is counted through the pivot. A link whose far end is filed on no entity, or on different ones, does not count.
  - **Not counted.** The derived lifespan (`Rules.Derived`): it is a Promote suggestion helper, not evidence, and the events it is built from already count as links. Conflicts and unknowns change nothing and are not collected. Source quality and independence do not weigh in.
  - **Two weights.** Zero or one counted fact is a weak (thin) line; two or more is a strong (thick) one. A shared person with no matching fact still draws a thin line, since sharing the person is itself the link (user, 2026-11-07). The cutoff, 2, is the one knob, in the match registry's record-links section. Any line joins two graphs; nothing sets a stronger bar for grouping.
  - **Refused** (user, 2026-11-07): one arc per pair of graphs instead of one per shared person, since the number of arcs is the data; counting a marriage and the spouse link it implies as two facts.
- **The view is a circular link diagram** (user, 2026-11-07; answers open question 12). The person's Evidence graphs sit around a circle and each line is an arc between two of them; two graphs that share several people get several arcs. Each graph is labelled with its graph ref, the one the Sources section's graph lines already show, so no numbering is added. Hovering a graph's line in the Sources list highlights its place and arcs on the circle, and hovering it on the circle highlights its line in the list, so the researcher knows which graph to go and look at. The view shows no data about a line: no label, tooltip or detail on hover or click; the arcs and their weights are the data. Brief S13-D10 draws it. Binds S13-28.
